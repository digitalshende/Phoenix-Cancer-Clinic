Colorectal Cancer Surgery

Colon & Rectal Cancer Care

Colorectal cancer (cancer of the colon or rectum) is one of the most treatable cancers when detected early. Surgery is the primary treatment, and the exact procedure depends on the size, location, and stage of the tumor.

At Phoenix Cancer Clinic, Dr. Khadkaban specializes in both traditional open surgeries and advanced laparoscopic (minimally invasive) techniques to remove colorectal tumors, ensuring complete cancer clearance while helping you return to your normal life as quickly as possible.

Signs, Symptoms & Risk Factors

Don't ignore the warning signs

When to see a doctor:

  • A persistent change in your bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or narrowing of the stool) lasting more than a few weeks.
  • Rectal bleeding or blood in your stool (bright red or very dark).
  • Persistent abdominal discomfort, such as cramps, gas, or pain.
  • A feeling that your bowel doesn't empty completely.
  • Unexplained weight loss or fatigue.

Major Risk Factors:

  • Age: Risk increases significantly after age 50.
  • Diet: High in red/processed meats and low in fiber.
  • Lifestyle: Obesity, sedentary lifestyle, smoking, and heavy alcohol use.
  • Family History: A history of colorectal cancer or polyps in close relatives.

Surgical Procedures Offered

Tailored surgery based on tumor location

Right/Left Hemicolectomy

If the tumor is located in the right or left side of the colon, this procedure is performed. It involves removing the affected portion of the colon along with nearby lymph nodes, and then reconnecting the healthy ends of the bowel (anastomosis).

Anterior Resection (AR)

Often used for tumors in the upper rectum or sigmoid colon. The surgeon removes the diseased section of the rectum and sigmoid colon, and then performs a primary anastomosis (rejoining the healthy ends). This procedure often preserves normal bowel function without the need for a permanent bag.

Abdominoperineal Resection (APR)

Reserved for very low rectal cancers located near the anus. It is a more extensive surgery involving the removal of the rectum, anus, and surrounding tissues. Because the anal sphincter is removed, this procedure requires a permanent colostomy (stoma).

Understanding Colostomies & Stoma Care

The idea of a colostomy bag can be frightening. However, it is important to know that a colostomy is a life-saving procedure that allows you to resume your daily activities. With modern stoma care products, the bag is discreet, odor-free, and easy to manage.

At Phoenix Cancer Clinic, we don't just perform the surgery; we provide comprehensive stoma care education and support. Our team will teach you or your caregiver exactly how to clean, change, and manage the colostomy before you leave the hospital, ensuring you feel confident and independent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answering your concerns

Will I definitely need a colostomy bag?

No. The majority of colon and upper rectal cancers can be treated with surgery that reconnects the bowel, avoiding a permanent bag. Only very low rectal tumors (where the sphincter muscle is involved) require a permanent colostomy (APR).

How will my diet change after colon surgery?

Initially, you will be on a clear liquid diet and progress slowly. You may be advised to avoid high-fiber foods, carbonated drinks, and gas-producing foods for a few weeks to allow your bowel to heal. We provide a detailed post-operative dietary guide.

Is colorectal surgery done with keyhole (laparoscopic) techniques?

Yes, whenever possible. Laparoscopic surgery for colon and rectal cancer offers smaller incisions, less pain, quicker return of bowel function, and a shorter hospital stay. Dr. Khadkaban will evaluate your specific case to determine if you are a suitable candidate.
