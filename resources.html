Patient Resources

Supporting You Through Your Journey

A cancer diagnosis brings many questions. At Phoenix Cancer Clinic, we believe that informed patients make empowered decisions. We have compiled essential resources, checklists, and answers to frequently asked questions to help you and your family navigate this journey with confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common concerns

How does AI help in my treatment?

AI analyzes your medical reports, scans, and global research databases to give Dr. Khadkaban data-driven insights. This ensures the most accurate diagnosis, precise staging of the cancer, and a highly personalized treatment plan tailored to your specific condition.

What should I bring to my first appointment?

Please bring all previous medical records, biopsy reports, radiology scans (CDs/films), a list of current medications (including supplements), and any relevant family medical history. Having these ready helps us create a complete picture of your health.

Is laparoscopic cancer surgery safe?

Yes. When performed by an experienced surgeon like Dr. Khadkaban, it is highly safe and often results in better outcomes, less pain, and faster recovery than traditional open surgery. It is a highly effective method for removing many types of tumors.

How long will I be in the hospital?

The length of your stay depends on the type of surgery. Minimally invasive (laparoscopic) surgeries often require a 2-4 day stay, while complex open surgeries may require 5-7 days. Dr. Khadkaban will give you a specific estimate before your procedure.

Will I need chemotherapy or radiation after surgery?

This depends entirely on the pathology report of the tumor removed during surgery. If there is a risk of microscopic spread, we may recommend adjuvant therapy. We will discuss this in detail during your post-operative follow-up.

Can I get a second opinion here?

Absolutely. We encourage patients to seek clarity. Dr. Khadkaban frequently provides second opinions for patients who want to confirm their diagnosis or explore different surgical approaches before committing to a treatment plan.

Pre-Surgery Preparation

How to prepare for a safe and successful surgery

Medical & Physical Prep

  • Complete all tests: Finish all required blood tests, ECG, and pre-anesthetic checkups as instructed.
  • Fasting: Follow fasting instructions strictly before surgery (usually no food or drink after midnight).
  • Medications: Inform your doctor about all medications. You may need to stop blood thinners a week before surgery.
  • Quit Smoking: If you smoke or chew tobacco, stopping at least 2 weeks before surgery significantly improves healing.

Logistical & Day-Of Prep

  • Arrange transport: Arrange for a family member or friend to drive you to and from the hospital.
  • Pack comfortable clothing: Bring loose-fitting, front-buttoning clothes (easier to put on after surgery) and comfortable footwear.
  • Bring essentials: Pack your ID, insurance documents, phone charger, and toiletries.
  • Leave valuables at home: Leave jewelry, large amounts of cash, and other valuables at home.

Post-Surgery Care & Recovery

Guidelines to help you heal safely at home

Diet & Nutrition

Start with light, easily digestible foods. Stay hydrated. Follow any specific dietary guidelines given by Dr. Khadkaban or our nutritionist. Avoid heavy, spicy, or gas-producing foods initially.

Wound Care

Keep the surgical site clean and dry. Follow instructions on when you can shower. Watch for signs of infection like increased redness, swelling, or discharge. Do not apply any home remedies to the wound.

Activity & Rest

Gentle walking is encouraged to prevent blood clots. However, avoid lifting heavy objects, strenuous exercise, or driving until cleared by the doctor. Prioritize sleep and rest.

Follow-Up Visits

Attend all scheduled follow-up appointments. These are crucial for checking your healing, removing stitches (if necessary), and reviewing the final pathology report.

When to Call the Doctor Immediately

While recovery is usually smooth, contact the clinic immediately if you experience:

  • Fever above 101°F (38.3°C) or chills.
  • Excessive bleeding or soaking through bandages.
  • Severe, uncontrolled pain.
  • Shortness of breath or chest pain.
  • Persistent nausea or vomiting.
  • Redness, warmth, or pus at the surgical site.
