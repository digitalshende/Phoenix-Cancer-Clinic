Gastrointestinal Cancers

Colorectal, Stomach, & Liver Cancers

Cancers of the digestive system, including stomach (gastric), colon, rectum, liver, and pancreatic cancers, require highly complex surgical interventions. The gastrointestinal tract is a delicate system, and surgery in this area demands absolute precision.

At Phoenix Cancer Clinic, we specialize in both traditional open surgeries and advanced laparoscopic (keyhole) surgeries for these conditions, ensuring the best possible oncological outcomes while prioritizing your comfort and recovery.

Signs, Symptoms & Risk Factors

Listening to your digestive health

When to see a doctor:

  • Persistent indigestion, heartburn, or stomach pain.
  • Unexplained weight loss and loss of appetite.
  • Blood in your stool or black, tarry stools.
  • Changes in bowel habits (diarrhea or constipation) lasting more than a few weeks.
  • Jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes).
  • Feeling full very quickly when eating.

Major Risk Factors:

  • Diet & Lifestyle: High intake of processed meats, low fiber diet, obesity, and alcohol consumption.
  • Smoking: Increases the risk of stomach and pancreatic cancers.
  • Infections: H. pylori bacteria (stomach) and Hepatitis B/C (liver).
  • Family History: Genetic syndromes that increase the risk of GI cancers.

Procedures Offered

Complex surgeries performed with expert precision

Gastrectomy

Removal of part (partial) or all (total) of the stomach. This procedure is commonly used to treat stomach (gastric) cancer. Our team provides comprehensive dietary and nutritional support post-surgery to help you adapt to your new digestive system.

Hepatectomy

Surgical removal of liver tumors. The liver has a remarkable ability to regenerate, meaning a significant portion can be safely removed if it contains cancer. This requires meticulous surgical planning and AI-assisted imaging to ensure safety.

Whipple's Operation

Also known as a Pancreaticoduodenectomy. It is a highly complex procedure for tumors in the pancreas, bile ducts, and duodenum. It involves removing the head of the pancreas, the gallbladder, part of the small intestine, and the bile duct.

Frequently Asked Questions

Understanding your recovery

How will my diet change after gastrointestinal surgery?

Depending on the procedure (especially a Gastrectomy or Whipple's), your digestive system will need time to adjust. You will likely need to eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid certain foods initially. Our clinic provides dedicated nutritional counseling to guide you through this transition safely.

Is laparoscopic surgery safe for cancer removal?

Yes. When performed by an experienced surgical oncologist like Dr. Khadkaban, laparoscopic surgery is highly safe and effective for many GI cancers. It follows the same strict oncological principles as open surgery, ensuring complete tumor removal and proper lymph node clearance.

What is the recovery time for a Whipple's operation?

A Whipple's operation is a major surgery. The hospital stay is typically 7 to 10 days. Full recovery at home can take 1 to 2 months. It requires careful monitoring, dietary adjustments, and follow-up visits to ensure proper healing of the digestive tract.
